Cyberpunk Camcorder

01Why I built it

I was inspired by the trailers for Cyberpunk: Edgerunners season 2 that came out over the summer, and I thought it would be fun to dedicate some time to it. I made it with spare parts I had lying around, mainly the ESP32 camera, which became the main limitation for the camcorder.

This is where I learned about filler primer and other ways to make 3D prints come out cleaner and more polished.

02The hard part

I did spend some time on hinge designs, but the real challenge wasn't hardware. It was software and decorating. Most of my time went into reworking how the display receives the camera feed while saving on RAM.

03How it works

  • Two boards. The camera board runs its own Wi-Fi access point, streams video and takes commands (capture, start and stop recording, list and delete files). The main board joins that network and does everything you see.
  • Viewfinder. The main board decodes each JPEG frame block by block and pushes it to a 320 x 240 display, with the HUD layered on top.
  • Effects. Scanline, bloom, VHS and neon looks, drawn into each decoded block before it reaches the screen.
  • Controls. Three buttons drive the menus, a settings page that saves between power-ups, and a gallery for photos and video.
  • Power. A single LiPo cell through a boost converter, with the voltage read through a divider so the OS knows how much charge is left.

05Shot on it

Every photo below came straight off the camcorder at its native 320 x 240, with no editing. Most are from around dusk, when the small sensor turns everything blue and grainy.

06Design decisions

Battery levels that hold still

A LiPo sags every time the camera draws a burst of current, so a single reading jumps between levels. The OS sets each level by voltage with hysteresis: LOW at 3.70 V, CRIT at 3.50 V, EMPTY at 3.35 V, and a level only clears once the cell is 0.08 V above it, after a 20-second filter.

Save the clip before the battery dies

At CRIT the camcorder stops recording on its own so the file closes cleanly. At EMPTY it shows a shutdown screen and drops into deep sleep instead of browning out and corrupting the card.

A HUD that never flickers

Drawing the HUD straight onto the screen made it flash every time a video frame landed. Now it draws into an off-screen sprite and goes out in one push, and menus repaint only the rows that change.
