01Goal

After the biped, I wanted a robot that stands on its own without active balance, so I can focus on gait. Four legs give it a stable base, and three joints per leg let it walk, turn and adjust its height.

02Where it stands

This one is a work in progress, waiting for funding. The full design is done in Fusion, and I've built and tested one leg, which already runs the step cycle the robot will use to turn in place. The rest of the parts I'm saving up to buy.

03How it works

  • The leg. Three servos: roll swings the leg sideways, the hip lifts it, and the knee bends it.
  • Turning in place. Each leg loops four phases. Lift the foot, swing it toward the turn while it's in the air, plant it, then push it back to center. The push turns the body, because the planted foot can't slide.
  • Power. The servos run from a separate 5 to 6 V supply that shares ground with the ESP32. Each MG996R can pull 1.5 to 2.5 A under load, far more than the board's 5 V pin can give.
